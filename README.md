# SnapEyes

Iris art from a phone photo. Marketing page at `/` (React + Vite + Tailwind) and the real studio at `/try`.

## How `/try` works

1. **Capture** (`src/try/TryApp.tsx`): the phone's own camera via `<input capture>`, or a gallery pick of 3-5 shots. There is no in-page live camera: a browser camera stream gives much softer photos than the camera app (no multi-frame processing, no telephoto switch, poor close focus). The photo never leaves the browser at full size: a 1600 px copy goes to analysis, then only the iris square (max 1400 px) is sent on.
2. **`POST /api/analyze`**: Gemini vision returns iris/pupil/glare boxes as JSON, a Daugman-style limbus fit refines the circle, size and sharpness are measured. Verdict: `good` (iris >= 500 px and sharp), `ok`, `weak`, with concrete tips.
3. **`POST /api/deglare`**: disk mask, Real-ESRGAN x4 (ONNX, CPU) when the iris is small, glare mask (relative brightness + vision boxes), and a Gemini image edit that rebuilds only the reflection area, blended back through a feathered mask.
4. **`POST /api/enhance`**: `faithful` = conservative restoration prompt with `thinking_level: high` plus a fidelity guard (low-frequency SSIM against the input; below 0.75 falls back to the faithful upscale). `artistic` = macro re-interpretation for weak photos. With consent the crop, result and metrics are stored to Vercel Blob (`eyes/<session>/…`) as training memory. The page never gets the clean result (`api/_lib/preview.py`): `image` is an 800 px display copy with the preview watermark across the iris, `sealed` the clean 1024 px JPEG encrypted and authenticated with a server-only key (HMAC-SHA256 keystream and tag, 48 h expiry), `sealed_sizes` the same at 768 and 560 px for 3-8 eyes. The order draft (`api/order.py`) opens `sealed` and stores the clean bytes, so `/api/master_eye` gets exactly the enhance output as its preview.
5. **`POST /api/compose`**: takes the sealed irises (opened on the server), PIL composition on the style backgrounds in `api/_assets/bg` with Cinzel/Plus Jakarta typography and a preview watermark, returned at 1024 px. The watermark's words are drawn at the display copy's strength on every iris disc (`iris.WATERMARK_IRIS`), so the artwork and its "Save preview" file carry no clean iris either. Only `sealed` (the clean 1024 px original) opens into an order's preview; the smaller `sealed_sizes` copies open only here.

`GET /api/health` reports what is configured on a deployment.

## Languages and markets

The site speaks English, German, Lithuanian and Hungarian. The language and the market are two separate things, both taken from the link and neither moving the other: `?lang=` picks the language, `?m=` the market (`eu`, `lt`, `hu`, `au`; currency and prices in `api/_lib/markets.py`). Nothing redirects. A page's language is, in this order: `?lang=`, the visitor's earlier choice (localStorage `snapeyes.lang`), the market's own language (`lt` Lithuanian, `hu` Hungarian, set in `markets.py`), the browser's language, English (`src/shared/lang.ts`). The Australian market is English and German only, because the Australian legal texts are.

- The words: the landing's are `src/landing/copy/<lang>.json` (one file per language, same keys; `src/landing/copy*.ts` keep only the label of the legal pages' language switch and the sentences /try and the terms repeat), `src/try/copy*.ts`, `src/order/copy*.ts` (English and German in the main file, `*.lt.ts` and `*.hu.ts` beside it, typed by the English dictionary: a missing key fails `tsc`). The legal texts are `src/legal/docs/*.ts` with `*.lt.ts` and `*.hu.ts`; the editions (EU, Australian, Hungarian with the forint prices) are `src/legal/editions.ts`, and which languages an edition has is `EDITION_LANGS` in `src/shared/legal.ts` and `api/_lib/pay.py`.
- The server: `api/analyze.py` (`TEXT_DE`, `TEXT_LT` in `api/_lib/analyze_lt.py`, `TEXT_HU` in `analyze_hu.py`), `api/_lib/iris.py` (error sentences, `WATERMARK_TEXT`), `api/_lib/pay.py` and `withdraw.py` (emails and receipts, with the Lithuanian and Hungarian texts in `pay_lt.py`, `pay_hu.py`, `withdraw_lt.py`, `withdraw_hu.py`). The order confirmation email carries the legal texts of the order's edition and language from `/legal/order-mail.json`, which the build writes.
- `npm run check:texts` (it also runs before every build) refuses: a Lithuanian or Hungarian dictionary with other keys than the English one, an untranslated English sentence, a dash, a missing sentence for a photo block reason, a legal text missing in a language of its edition, terms that print another currency's prices or do not name every contract language, a consent version in the future or different from the server's, and a withdrawal waiver or statement that is not the same words on the page and on the server. A new function in a copy dictionary needs sample arguments in `scripts/check_texts.mjs`. Hungary is sellable (`"selectable": 1`) only together with its legal edition; `npm run check:prices` enforces that.
- Adding a language means: its dictionaries, its legal texts, `LANGS` in `src/shared/lang.ts` and `api/_lib/pay.py`, its words in the terms' contract-languages sentence in every language, `hreflang` in the five indexable pages and `public/sitemap.xml`, its watermark (measured on every canvas), its analyze sentences, its emails and a new consent version.
- `npm run check:texts` also keeps the two lists honest that nobody reads by hand: a Lithuanian or Hungarian string that is word for word the English or German one, English or German function words in any Lithuanian or Hungarian string (the dictionaries, the legal texts, the built legal pack and the customer sentences in `api/_lib/*_lt.py` and `*_hu.py`), and a consent text that changed under the same consent version (`CONSENT_FINGERPRINTS` in `scripts/check_texts.mjs`: changing a consent text needs a new `CONSENT_VERSION` in `api/_lib/pay.py` and `WITHDRAWAL_CONSENT_VERSION` in `src/shared/legal.ts` together, and the new fingerprint, which the check prints). A word that is also a real Lithuanian or Hungarian word stays off its lists on purpose (`WORDS_EN`, `WORDS_DE`); a word that has to stay English goes into `ENGLISH_OK`.

## Price experiments

Price tests are defined in one place, `api/_lib/experiments.py` (every variant carries its FULL ladder per market; the variant `control` is the standard ladder of `api/_lib/markets.py`; a finished test is kept with `"retired": 1`). Nothing runs until the owner starts it in the admin page "Kainų testai" (`/admin#tests`); the state is `ops/experiments/<key>.json` in the private bucket and every start and stop is in the admin audit log (with the risks the owner accepted). Off, the site behaves exactly as before, and the browser keeps nothing.

- The page asks `GET /api/checkout` without any id, as it always did. Only while a test runs does the answer also name the markets that run one (`exp_markets`). A visitor of such a market (who has not opted out with `?pricetest=off`, the link in the privacy policy) gets a random anonymous id in localStorage (`snapeyes.vid`, 90 days at most, deleted again when no test runs) and asks once more with the id in the header `X-Snapeyes-Visitor` (never in a URL). The server derives the variant from the id (sticky), answers with that variant's ladders and a signed assignment token (HMAC under the ticket secret). `POST /api/checkout` prices the order from the verified token only (`api/_lib/abtest.py`); no valid token means the standard ladder, and a page that sends the price it showed (`shown`) gets `409 price_changed` instead of a different charge.
- The experiment, variant and ladder are recorded in `order.json` (checkout.experiment), the Stripe metadata (`exp`, `exp_var`, `exp_lad`) and `paid.json` (experiment with the ladder that applied); `session_matches` refuses a paid session whose amount is not the price of its own ladder, and the confirmation email names the price list that applied. Because the ladder travels with the session, editing, retiring or deleting a test never makes an open checkout unpayable. Rule of thumb: stop the test, wait 24 hours, then change prices or definitions.
- Anonymous events (kind `exp`: visit, preview, checkout, paid) give the visitors, previews and payment pages per variant; paid orders and revenue come from the order records (live payments, refunded and withdrawn ones apart). The admin page shows the funnel, conversion, revenue per visitor and average order in the test's currency, significance tests (conversion, the affected orders, revenue per visitor; a p-value only with at least 10 paid orders and 100 visitors per arm, and a verdict only once the planned size is reached), the sample size needed, orders that took no part, and a warning when a ladder would sell at a loss (`COSTS` in the same file).
- `npm run check:prices` (and every build) validates every variant ladder (`scripts/check_experiments.mjs`) and that the page's price rule agrees with the server's. Never edit a ladder that has run: add a new experiment key instead.

## Styles

Which styles exist is defined in one place, `api/_lib/styles_registry.py`: the ids, brand names, slugs, eye counts, layouts, the stage ceiling, the price class (`black` or `art`), the gate policy and the sizes a render may work at. How each style is drawn is in `api/_lib/styles_engine.py`, which only Python reads (the public file is imported by the pages as text and ships in their JavaScript). The server asks `api/_lib/catalogue.py` (`known`, `orderable`, `previewable`, `stage_of`, `price_class`, `engine_for`, `tiles_for`, `pick_for`, ...), the pages ask `src/shared/styles.ts`; nobody else keeps a list of style ids, a style name or the rule that makes a style cost the black price.

- The stage in the file is the CEILING (`planned`, `lab`, `preview`, `live`, `retired`); the owner's override in the admin page can only lower it (see "The style switch" below), and `EFFECTIVE_DEFAULT` holds every style of the v3 engine at `preview` until his tick. The six legacy ids (`legacy: 1`) were retired by the cutover (WP18, "The catalogue switch" below); the release-1 styles have live ceilings, the held ones are `lab`, the reserved ones `planned`, and a style whose engine module is not in the repository is never routed to, whatever its stage says (`catalogue.engine_built`).
- `npm run check:styles` (and every build; item 15 ties the landing's gallery tiles to the registry) refuses a registry that breaks the schema, two ids with one slug, a heart or a pet symbol in an id, a style id written in any other file of `api/`, `src/` or `scripts/` (the allow list is in `scripts/check_styles.mjs`), a copy dictionary keyed by style id that does not match, a layout without a name, and price rules that disagree; it prints the registry hash (12 hex digits, also `catalogue.registry_hash()`). The rules of the texts (no number of styles or of eyes in a string, the run-time tokens `{styles}` and `{max}` of the landing's pricing rows) are on (`WP12_RULES`) and read the landing's `src/landing/copy/<lang>.json`.
- A new style is a registry entry first: add it to both files, run `npm run check:styles`, and raise its stage only in a reviewed change.
- The words for the layouts ("Side by side", "Nebeneinander", "Greta", "Egymás mellett": how the irises of an artwork are arranged) are in one place too, `api/_lib/layout_names.py`, in English, German, Lithuanian and Hungarian for every layout id a style takes. The e-mails read them through `catalogue.layout_name(lang, id)`, the picker and the order page through `layoutName(lang, id)` of `src/shared/layouts.ts`. A new layout id needs its four words there in the same change (the build refuses the registry otherwise, and refuses a word no style takes and any other table that names layouts). The words of the v3 layouts are drafts and wait for a native reading of the Lithuanian and Hungarian ones.

## Style engine: the core, the plates and the bundle

`api/_lib/styles/` is the package the v3 engines are drawn with. It imports nothing heavy (importing the package takes a few milliseconds and loads no engine: a family is imported on first use, `EngineNotBuilt` otherwise), so `enhance`, `checkout` and the webhook can use the profile and the plan without carrying an engine.

- `core.py`, `layouts.py`: the foundation of the design rounds, ported verbatim (the float canvas, splat, blur, the tone map, dither, `Rand` and the seeds, the ring colours and colour class, the disc that is pasted LAST, the solo layouts). `scripts/styles_tests/test_engine_core.py` replays 86 golden cases recorded on the prototype and every one is equal: a port is verbatim when its hashes are. The studio writes nothing on an artwork, so the prototype's tagline, footer, caption and watermark are gone; the process-wide working size became `Iris(max_side)`. Caches are `BoundedCache` (a warm instance renders for hours: no module level dict may grow).
- `text.py`: the customer's own words (names, date, family name) and the draw log that check T7 reads. `unsupported(text)` lists the letters the artwork font cannot draw (read from the font's cmap table, no dependency), so a name is refused before checkout and no empty box reaches a delivered file.
- `selfcheck.py`: the run-time checks of the design brief (T1 the iris is byte for byte the graded disc, T2 pupils clear, T3 visible share, T4 black share, T6, T7, T12), about half a second at 4096 px; `costs.py`: what a design costs in seconds and megabytes, baked from the measured spike (`step_need`, `break_even_factor`, `est_mb`, `assess`). `STYLE_SLOW_CPU` (the factor from the admin action `cpu_probe`) and `STYLE_FUNCTION_MEM_MB` (the project's function memory) are read from the environment.
- `plates.py`, `atlas.py`: the plate library, read only. `api/_lib/plates_registry.py` is BAKED OFFLINE by `scripts/bake_plates_registry.py` from the registries of the design rounds (nothing is fitted, scanned or written at run time; a function's folder is read-only): 221 plates, 169 usable, each with `since` and `until` (the library is append-only: a plate added later never changes the pick of an older `pv`, a retired plate still answers an old one). The 1K files (37 MiB) and the two sprite atlases (6.8 MiB) are in `api/_assets/plates` and `api/_assets/atlas`; the 4K files live in private storage under `plates/v1/<family>/<file>` and are fetched by id, checked against the registry's size and sha256, cached in `/tmp/snapeyes_plates` (written atomically, 256 MiB cap, least recently used out; `STYLE_PLATE_CACHE`, `STYLE_PLATE_CACHE_MB`). A missing or wrong 4K plate raises `PlateUnavailable`: the order is held, no other plate is ever used. `pick`, `pick_n`, `plates` and `Plate.place` are the prototype registry's loader half (the pick is `min(candidates, key=sha256(seed | family | id))`), proved equal on 14 groups of cases. Two JET plates had a legend drawn into the picture by the generator (a pair of checkerboard scale bars; three particle size icons with "(mm)"): `RETOUCH` in the bake script fills those rectangles with black in the 1K file and fits the plate again (the legend had moved the source point of one plate by 1.8 percent of its width); `v3plates` proves every collision plate's fit is the fit of the pixels it ships. No pixel test can find such a legend (every plate holds detached debris), so a family is looked at before it is baked: `python scripts/plate_sheet.py <family> --out sheet.png` (and `--strips 140 --gain 3` for the bottom edge).
- `scripts/upload_plates.py` puts the 4K files into storage (a dry run unless `--yes`; idempotent; never deletes; a stored object that is not the registry's file is reported and left alone). The first release needs `--release1` (Powder Burst's CLOUD, Splash's CROWN, Vortex's SPIRAL and the DUST plates that Universe Echo lays on its tall canvas: 109 plates, about 413 MiB, to be read against the storage plan before it runs). It reads no key itself: `api/_lib/store.py` takes the service key and the bucket from the environment as everywhere else.
- Where the plates are: `vercel.json` has one entry per function and only `compose`, `master_compose`, `order` and `admin` (the four that render) keep the plates and atlases; the other seven exclude them, and every entry keeps `suites/**` out. `node scripts/bundle_report.mjs` says what each function holds. `npm run check:styles` (and every build) also refuses a plate file that is missing, changed or unlisted, an atlas that is not the registry's, a style shown to customers without its tile images, a rendering function that would pass the 235 MiB tripwire (the files of `api/` plus the recorded size of the Python packages), a `vercel.json` with a glob or the wrong exclusions, and any image of the repository that is byte for byte a reference work or a calibration iris (`scripts/hygiene_denylist.json`, made by `scripts/make_denylist.py`: hashes only).
- `GET /api/health` answers `styles` and `plates_4k` (booleans): the registries agree, and the 4K plates the visible styles fetch are in storage (nothing is asked while no visible style fetches one). The health function carries no plates by design, so whether the FILES are in the bundle of the rendering functions is the build's check and the admin action `plates_status`, which runs in a rendering function.
- The suites use no real eye: `scripts/styles_tests/synth_iris.py` draws procedural irises (fibres, round, slit and bar pupils, three colour classes, an eyelid wedge and a lash fan for the gate). `record_*.py` run the prototype once to record the goldens (they need the scratch tree: `SNAPEYES_SCRATCH_Y3`, `SNAPEYES_SCRATCH_PLATES`); the suites only replay. The suites `v3core` and `v3plates` run them.

### The singles family (`api/_lib/styles/singles`, `api/_lib/styles/matter.py`)

Six single-eye designs: Clean Iris, Powder Burst, Splash, Elements, Radiance and Celestial Gold (the owner-approved variant A, an even corona; the old design is not what ships). They are the design rounds' own code, ported verbatim: `scripts/styles_tests/port_singles.py` makes every file from the scratch prototype by a listed set of edits (imports, plates and atlases read through `styles.plates` and `styles.atlas`, bounded caches, the text drawer of `styles.text`) and `v3single` proves both halves: the committed files are the scratch plus those edits (the step A edits and the one step B set, `STEP_B` in the same tool), and the pictures are the scratch's own, byte for byte, when the old seed is asked for (`singles_cases.py` is the list of cases, `record_goldens_scratch.py` records them on the scratch code into `data/stepA/`, 72 hashes with the plate picks and seeds and 24 hashes of the owner's own calibration eyes: hashes only).

**The seed (WP5B, decision C9 step B, `api/_lib/styles/seeds.py`).** A picture is a function of the iris pixels, of WHICH eye it shows (its `eye_id`, the id of the sealed profile) and of the plan's seed key (registry id of the style, design used, ground, clean flag, layout id, the three options swap, rotate and look, the plates version `pv`), and of nothing else: not the names, the date, the canvas or its size, not the bytes the eye arrives in. The preview, the order's draft and the master of one eye carry the same id, so they seed alike; `resolve` names the seed and the plate or plates the picture will draw from before the render (checkout verifies their 4K files are in storage); every plate pick takes the spec's `pv`, so a plate added to the library later cannot change an older order. What the plan freezes (the liquid of a splash, from the sealed profile) is what the preview and the master draw, and the master checks the seed and the plates it drew against the plan before it stores anything (hold `picture_drift`; a master made from another preview than the plan's is held `eye_changed`). The seed change moved every design but Clean Iris (it draws no matter): the goldens were recorded again on this code (`record_goldens_repo.py`, `data/singles_goldens.json`, `singles_goldens_real.json`) in one commit with the reviewed diff (`diff_goldens.py [--pixels]`), the step A hashes are kept in `data/stepA/` and replayed with `opts seed_mode legacy`, which seeds the old way: byte for byte equal, which is the proof that the seed is the ONLY thing step B changed. `ENGINE_V` is 2. The admin laboratory has the same switch (the Sėkla menu of **Stilių laboratorija**), so a board can be looked at before and after.
- Contract (`api/_lib/styles/__init__.py`): `resolve(spec, profiles)` (the plan without a pixel), `preview(eyes, spec, size, watermark, check)` (a `Preview`: the clean render, the visible discs, the graded frame, the engine's facts, the times and, with `check`, the self checks T1, T6, T7, T12 and T4), `tiles(...)`; `styles.watermarked(preview)` is the one place a preview gets its watermark (an engine never draws it). The family's own entry is `render(design, eye, fmt, size, names, date)`.
- Nothing of the family is visible to customers: every single style is still at the `lab` ceiling. `api/compose.py` hands a one-eye request for a v3 style to the engine only when its effective stage is `preview` or `live`; the admin page sees the others in **Laboratorija > Stilių laboratorija** (the action `styles_lab`: one style on one restored iris, the site's restored sample, a file or the stored 4K eye of a lab test order; no image model, nothing stored; 4096 px comes back as a 1536 px view and a 1280 px window at 100 percent, with the self checks, the colour class, the seed (the customer's, or the old one for a before and after look), the plates picked, the times and the cost table's estimate).
- Run the replay with `bash suites/run_main.sh v3single`; with `SNAPEYES_SCRATCH_Y3` (the `wave-y3` folder of the scratch tree) it also replays the 4096 px pictures of the three plate styles through a local store of their 4K plates and checks the edits, and with `SNAPEYES_CALIB` the real eyes (LOCAL lines, not counted).

## The master plan: how a paid artwork is made (`api/_lib/styles/steps.py`)

Every paid artwork is made by a PLAN, one `art` step for every release-1 style (every design fits one 60 s call at the measured slow factor). The plan, the steps, the claims, the guards and the events are built all the same: they are what the order page, the order detail and the lab show, and a longer function limit collapses everything to single steps with no other change.

- **Records** (`orders/<order>/style/`, small JSON, removed 14 days after `delivery.json` by the daily clean-up): `plan.json` (the style, the design actually used, the layout, the canvas, the eyes' ids, the seed key, the steps with their estimated seconds and megabytes, `plan8`: 8 hex digits of everything that decides the picture and nothing that is only provenance), `done_<step>.json` (written LAST: time, CPU, the increase of the resident size, outputs, inputs, drift), `try_<step>.json` (kills, plate faults, error signatures, `open`), `<step>.lock` (the step's claim, taken over after the lease), `rerun.json` (the admin's rerun count). The artwork of a v3 style is `artwork_<sha256(plan8, rerun)[:16]>.jpg`: an unrelated registry edit never renames an unfinished order's file; a legacy style keeps `master_compose`'s own digest and file.
- **One step through the state machine** (`run_step`): read the plan and the done records; wait for the two process guards (`guard.py`: one memory budget, one heavy render at a time); check the time and memory it needs; claim the step; set `open`; arm the watchdog; render; write the outputs; write the done record; `delivery.json`; clear `open`. A claim that finds `open` still set counts one KILL (the third holds the order); a storage error, a busy answer or a refusal before the claim never counts; a plan that can never fit (`costs.assess`: time over the work budget, memory over the budget, or a fresh invocation that cannot fit it) is a HOLD (`style_step_too_big`), never a busy hop; a second identical exception holds (`style_step_failed`); the second plate fault holds (`plate_unavailable`, the first is a 503 `plate_retry` that is not a `MAX_BUSY` hop); a plan made under another `ENGINE_V` holds (`engine_skew`); a master whose colour class is not the preview's holds (`class_changed`), and so does one whose pupil class (round, slit, bar) is not the sealed profile's (`pupil_changed`). A hold writes `review.json` and tells the owner once; the order is never made in another style.
- **No room** (`guard.Busy`): a step that finds another heavy render holding the instance's CPU or memory waits up to 2 s, then is answered 503 `room_retry` before it claims anything (it counts nothing). `retry_after` is how long the holder that frees room first still needs by its own estimate (`Busy.eta_s`), at least `ROOM_MIN_S` (5 s) and at most `ROOM_MAX_S` (40 s), `ROOM_DEFAULT_S` (10 s) when it declared none. The server's chain and the order page wait that long before they ask again (one busy hop, but spaced), so a second paid order is not out of its ten busy hops in the few seconds the waits take. `busy_retry` (no time left in this call) is unchanged: a new call has fresh time, so it is asked again at once.
- **The watchdog**: nothing runs after a killed function but the order page (when open), the status nudge and the daily run, so the step arms two relay hops of the server's own chain (`maker.arm_watchdog`) that wait out its claim and then retake it: a killed step is retaken about `duration.watchdog_total` seconds later with no page open. A relay that wakes to a finished order stops.
- **One duration constant** (`api/_lib/duration.py`, `DURATION_S` 60): the work budget (`iris.BUDGET`), every lease (`LEASE_STALE`, `COMPOSE_STALE`, an eye's and a step's claim, the clean-up's lock), `FRESH` and the chain's waits derive from it, and `scripts/check_styles.mjs` item 13 holds every `maxDuration` of `vercel.json` equal to it: raising the limit is one coordinated change, never one number in one file. `PROBE_HOPS` is the longest planned chain (eyes + the plan's steps + the ready email) plus 4, 14 today.
- **Admin**: `order_steps` (the plan, every step with its records, the claims, the capacity at the factor in force, the eyes' ids and sealed gate results, the artwork's record), `rerun_step` (a new file beside the delivered one, never deleting it), `recompose` (the same file when no input changed), `lab_steps` (the master steps of a style on the 4096 px masters stored for a lab test order: no payment, no image model; this is how VE1 reads the real time and memory of a design). The order detail and the laboratory show the step table. `python scripts/order_admin.py status ORDER` prints the plan.
- **Memory**: `peak_mb` in the events and records is the INCREASE of the process's resident size over the step (`VmRSS`, sampled while the step runs), `hwm_mb` the instance's own high-water mark beside it, labelled as that: `ru_maxrss` never falls on a warm instance and would say nothing about one step.
- `ENGINE_V` (`api/_lib/styles/__init__.py`) changes if and only if a golden changes: `scripts/styles_tests/data/engine_v.json` records it with the hashes of the golden files and `v3steps` compares them (`python scripts/styles_tests/test_steps.py --record-engine-v` records the new ones together with the reviewed diff).
- Run the suite with `bash suites/run_main.sh v3steps` (about two minutes: it drives the chain over HTTP with a stub for the image model; a few 4096 px masters are drawn by the real singles engine).

## The style switch: the stage the owner controls, and the numbers beside it (`api/_lib/stage_overrides.py`, `api/_lib/style_stats.py`)

The stage in the registry is the CEILING. The EFFECTIVE stage of a style for n eyes is the lower of the ceiling and the owner's override, which he sets in the admin page and which needs no deploy (`catalogue.stage_of`). An override can only lower or restore up to the ceiling: the action refuses a stage above it (409 `above_ceiling`) and the reader ignores one that a hand edit put in the file. `planned` and `retired` styles cannot be switched (409 `not_switchable`). A style becomes `live` only after the owner has recorded his own look at its final renders (check L1) and the independent score of the art director (L0) or his written waiver of it, for every eye count asked for (409 `needs_ticks`, `missing` says which, `l0` says where the score stands); ordering itself is the other switch, `pay.ordering_problem()`. **L0 is a score, not a tick** (spec 1.6.1): `evidence {L0: {mean, min_axis, by}}` with mean at least 3.96, no axis under 3.8 and the scorer's name counts; a score below the bar is recorded and shown (`l0` of the range: `pass`, `below_bar`, `incomplete`, `waiver`, or null; `l0_bar` of the catalogue gives the numbers) and does not count; below the bar only the written waiver does. A bare `tick {L0: true}` is a 400 (`tick {L0: false}` still takes a mark back).

- **One object**: `ops/styles/overrides.json` in the private bucket holds the override of every id, the ticks L0 to L11 (dated, attributed to the admin key's kind), the L0 waivers and the limits of the attention card, per eye count, with a revision number. Its single writer is the admin action `styles_override`, under a lock, and the page sends back the revision it saw (a second tab's change is a 409 `stale_view`, not a silent overwrite).
- **The read path** (`stage_overrides.source`, plugged into `catalogue._override_source`): one read of that object per instance per 30 seconds, however many times a request asks (every tile asks). A failed refresh is remembered for 5 seconds, so a storage that does not answer costs one slow call, not thirty. **Failing closed**: a storage error is never read as "no override". `catalogue.orderable` and `orderable_ids` are strict and raise `StorageError` (the checkout answers 503 `storage_busy`, nothing is created); `stage_of`, the tile list and the public catalogue fall back to the ceiling capped at preview, never live. No storage at all (the dev server) is not an error. `set_override_source(None)` is "no overrides" (the registry tests). `GET /api/checkout` and every page-facing question must use the lenient forms.
- **The cutover's hook**: `catalogue.EFFECTIVE_DEFAULT` is the stage a v3 style has until the owner's tick records another (the six legacy ids are never held to it). It is `"preview"` since WP18 (one line of the registry file, `EFFECTIVE_DEFAULT`), so only the owner's recorded tick makes a style orderable; `restore` gives the default, not the ceiling.
- **Actions** (all behind the admin key): `styles_catalogue` (the page's switch: every id, its ranges, ceiling, override, effective stage, ticks, waiver, what `live` lacks, `rev`, the price tests that run), `styles_override` (change, tick, waive; every stage change needs `confirm`; one revision and one entry of the audit log per change), `styles_audit` (`ops/styles/audit/<day>/<time>-<rand>.json`, newest first: who, what, why, before and after, the funnel numbers of that count at that moment with n, the price test, what he chose for the orders in flight), `styles_limits`, `styles_stats`; and the laboratory of a group, `styles_lab` with `eyes` (1 to 8 images) or `order` and `ns`: one tile of every style of that eye count, held ones too, clean, with the gate readout of the eyes (the gate does not withhold a tile there: the owner judges it by looking at what it lets through).
- **A running price test** (spec 4.4): a flip that changes what can be ordered needs `price_test_seen` while a test runs (409 `price_test_running` names the keys: the page shows "a price test is running: this flip changes its sample", the owner decides); the entry names the test, and the price tests page lists the flips made inside its window (`abtest.catalogue_changes`).
- **Orders in flight** (decision DE1): taking back a style that was orderable asks what happens to the paid orders being made. `in_flight` `hold` puts every paid order of the last 3 days that is still being made for that style and eye count in review (`review.json`, reason `style_rolled_back`, the owner clears it as any hold); `finish` leaves them. The default is hold when `reason_kind` is `quality`, finish otherwise. A Stripe page opened before the change and paid after it is not held (the render path ignores stages). The change is saved first and the hold never raises and never says more than was done: `held` is `{count, orders, checked, more, failed, unread, error, incomplete}`, each order read back after its `review.json` was written (`pay.mark_review` never raises, so a failed write would otherwise count as a hold), and when it stopped half way (`incomplete`: a storage error, an order record that could not be read, no time left) the SAME request again with `in_flight: hold` answers `result: same` and holds the rest (the orders held already are left alone; the retry has its own audit entry, kind `hold`). The reply's `audit_written` is false when the log line could not be stored (the change stands).
- **The numbers** (`styles_stats`, computed by `style_stats.report` from the usage events' day counts): the SET-level gate funnel per eye count and per colour class (a set is counted once, by its first request: the page marks repeats with `again: true`), first photo, pass, pass counting one retake (an upper bound: an event holds no set id), always with n; the opening line of spec 1.6.2 per count of two or more eyes (30 sets, 50 percent on the first photo, 75 percent with one retake: green or red with what is not met); demand for styles that cannot be bought yet (tiles looked at, large previews, clicks on the Soon button, checkouts refused); chosen against recommended, and by the set's eye class (`chosen_by_class`); the funnel after the preview by style and eye count (`conversion`: large previews while live, Stripe sessions started, paid, from the `checkout` events the checkout and the payment record send: `recorded` is false until they do) and ordered after failure per eye count (`after_failure`: the share of paid orders made on a set that failed the gate of its style); `qa_ok` false by style (`qa`); `busy_retry` by endpoint and style; render times p50 and p95 from a histogram against the cost table's estimate; errors, review rate, fallbacks (the share of stacked lenses per pair design), the gate per eye under each style's own rule; the attention card against the limits. A market or a language filter (one at a time) swaps in the slice tables for the tables that carry them (`filter.whole` lists the rest). The style audit log has the retention rule of `ops/audit` (24 months, `cleanup._audit`).
- **For the pages that send events** (WP11, WP12): compose accepts `again` (true on every request about eyes already asked about) and `action: "help"` with `route: "soon"`, `style`, `eyes` (a click on the buy button of a style that is Soon); a checkout that answers `409 style_unavailable` should name `style` and `eyes` in the body, and `events.answer` records the demand by itself. **WP12 must also send the `checkout` event** (not yet called by anything: until it is, `conversion` and `after_failure` read zero and say `recorded: false`): `events.record("checkout", stage="start", style=..., eyes=..., gate=<the set's gate result under the style's rule: ok, unknown or the failing eye's reason code>, lang=..., market=...)` after the Stripe session of an order was created, and the same with `stage="paid"` where the payment is recorded (`pay.record_paid`, once per order). No order id and no amount in it.
- Run the suite with `bash suites/run_main.sh v3admin` (about a minute: the read path and its cache with a moved clock, every refusal, the audit log, the events against a replay, the contact sheet of a group, the sentences, and a checkout that fails closed over HTTP).

## Checkout, e-mails and the legal texts: the order spec, the frozen plan, the words (`api/checkout.py`, `api/_lib/pay.py`, `api/_lib/words.py`, `src/legal`)

- **The spec of an order** (`pay.spec_from`): `{eyes, style, layout, names, title, lang, market}` and, when the order has them, `date` and `opts`. `names` is a LIST (one name per eye at most, 24 characters a name, 200 in all; the old string `"Anna;Max"` is still read), `date` one line of 20 characters, `opts` the options that APPLY to the style (`swap` for two eyes, `rotate` for three or more, a `look`: `catalogue.applied_opts`, the very rule `api/compose.py _tile_opts` follows for a preview, so a checkout and a preview of one choice make one plan). A NEW order for a style of the v3 engine has its words REFUSED when they break a limit or hold a letter the artwork font cannot draw (400 `bad_words {why, field, chars}`; a heart is one of them); an order for a legacy style keeps what that engine always did (no date, no options, the names cut at 200). A paid session's metadata and a recorded spec are read LENIENTLY (`words.py`: every old wire form, never a refusal): the metadata of a session made before this work (names as text, no date, no plan keys) is still a paid order.
- **`409 style_unavailable {style, eyes, why}`** (spec 2.9): the style is known but cannot be bought for these eyes now. `why`: `eyes` (it does not take that many), `stage` (it is not live: it opens soon, it was taken back in the admin page, or the LOOK the order draws is not live), `gate` and `reseal` (a hard style and an eye whose SEALED gate value failed or is missing: the draft's profile decides, nothing is measured again; an advisory style is bought on a failing eye and the failure is recorded), `bar_pupil` (the collision family draws no bar pupil; `catalogue._problem` is the one place it is judged), `plates` (a 4K plate the plan will draw is not in storage: found before payment, never after), `capacity` (the plan's time or memory need can never fit one call at the slow factor in force: a refusal that can never succeed is found here and not retried as busy after payment). Nothing is created, an earlier open session of the order is left alone, and `events.answer` counts the demand (a `help` event, route `blocked`). An id the registry does not know is still the old 400. A storage error that hides the owner's switch is `503 storage_busy`: checkout fails closed.
- **The plan is frozen at checkout** (C8): `checkout.freeze_plan` makes the plan of the order from the draft's sealed eye records (`steps.make_plan`; for the collision family from the pixels of the approved previews, `plan_irises`), stores it in `order.json` (`checkout.plan`, `checkout.engine {v, reg, pv}`, the set level gate result, whether the page's plan8 was compared) and writes its plates version, engine version and `plan8` to the Stripe metadata (`pv`, `ev`, `plan8`, beside `date`, `opts` and the names as one JSON string). The reply carries `plan8` and `engine`. The page's `plan8` (compose's reply says it) is only COMPARED: another value is `409 plan_changed {plan8, engine}` (retry true, nothing created) and the page shows the preview again; a request that sends none is not compared (recorded as `plan8_shown: false`). **One exception (the WP7B open item, taken in the review of WP12):** for the collision family compose reads the 768 or 560 px copies of three to eight eyes and checkout the 1024 px ones, so a choice that sits at its threshold (the woven or stacked lens, the front order, the hairline contacts: `frozen`, with the fallback and the design drawn) can fall the other way, and the page would be sent round a `409 plan_changed` that comes again at every retry. Compose therefore says `plan8_core` beside `plan8` (`steps.plan8_core`: the same hash without `steps.PIXEL_KEYS`, that is without what the pixels decided), the page sends both back, and when `plan8` differs, the core is the server's and the family decides from the pixels (`styles.wants_eyes`), the server's plan is the plan: the order is made, `checkout.plan8_note` is `pixel_choices` and `checkout.plan8_page` is what the page showed, the Stripe metadata and the master carry the server's plan8. A different layout, option, eye, eye count, plates or engine version, style or step has another core and is still `409 plan_changed`; a family that does not decide from the pixels never takes this road. What the customer sees is then the plan the master draws, not the picture of the smaller copy: on the three real eyes of the checks the plans were equal, so the road is an insurance and the order detail (`plan8_note`) is where to look for its use. `record_paid` writes the session's own `plan8` and engine to `paid.json`, and `steps.create_plan` uses the plan stored in `order.json` only when its plan8 is the paid session's (a second checkout that replaced it cannot swap what was paid for).
- **The words** (`api/_lib/words.py`, no numpy): the limits (the same numbers as `styles/text.py`, a test holds them equal), the lenient reading of every wire form, `meta_names` (the compact JSON of the list with `ensure_ascii` off, at most 480 characters: eight names of 24 non-ASCII letters are 217, the old `json_bytes` would need 1,100 against Stripe's 500) and `meta_opts`. The 200 total moved with it: the preview's names line and the paid file's both keep the whole lockup of eight names of 24 letters (213 characters, `words.LOCKUP_MAX`, was 60), and the legacy composer still gets the old `"Anna;Max"` string (`names_wire`). **The legacy preview and the legacy paid file draw ONE line** (the legacy composer draws the string as it is): `compose._legacy_names` is `names_wire(names_list(...))`, the very reading `spec_from` records and `names_wire` hands to `master_compose`, so `Mantas; Ruta` is `Mantas;Ruta` in both and a no-break space, a zero width character or a run of spaces cannot make them differ (the page's own `Anna;Max` is untouched; `v3checkout` section 9 holds the parity for 19 inputs).

## The picker: what `/try` shows after the restoration (`src/try/picker.ts`, `StylePicker.tsx`, `usePreviews.ts`, `names.ts`)

The result screen owns no list of styles: it renders what the server says of the customer's own eyes (`POST /api/compose`). For one set of eyes `usePreviews.ts` asks three things in this order: `styles: []` (the tile list, the recommended tile and each eye's gate result, no pixel), `style` at 1024 px (the style on screen), then `styles: [a, b]` at 480 px (the other tiles, two at a time; a skeleton until each arrives; a tile for the style on screen reuses its large preview). Every answer is checked against the eyes that are on screen now. `again: true` goes on every request after the first of a set and `retake` carries the eyes replaced since the last set, so the admin's gate funnel counts a set once.

`picker.ts` is pure (no React, no fetch, no registry): the group by the number of eyes (the landing's three: Just you, Two of you, Family), the style on screen (the customer's choice whenever the eyes can take it, else the recommended tile, else the first tile that can be drawn; a chosen style that does not take this many eyes gives way with one line, a saved choice that is simply not listed falls back without a word), the state of each tile (ready, Soon, held back by an eye, reseal, pupil), the retake state and the buy card state. Rules it keeps: a pick that is not live and available is ignored; a Soon tile has no price, no order button and no date; a group none of whose styles is live says so once instead of a chip per tile; the price is printed only on tiles of the one-eye group that can be bought now; the recommended tile is the server's (colour class, never price) and is never pre-ticked for purchase (the waiver is never ticked for the customer). `gate` (none, advisory, hard) and `rule` (lid, fill) are on every tile row of the compose reply so the page can name the eye to retake: a hard style that an eye fails is held back ("Retake eye N first"), an advisory style is still drawn and warned about under the picture, on the eye chip and above the waiver. Where nothing can be bought for a set (a pair or a trio that fails the gate) the retake card shows the two plain reasons, one capture tip and the way to ask the owner (the e-mail link, one counted click: `action: "help"`); a one-eye set always has an advisory style, so it never ends in a dead end. A set whose every style is held for its pupils (the collision styles refuse a bar pupil) has the same card: a pupil sentence, the open-eye tip, a retake and a remove button for each eye with that pupil and the manual route; a tile list with no style at all says so with the manual route, and the frame never waits for a picture that will not come. A style that can be bought whose look on screen only previews (Universe, Vortex) shows the Soon line naming the look and no price or button, the verdict of checkout's `look_orderable`; `v3picker` section 5 holds the page's buy state equal to the server's for every style, eye count and look.

The customer's own words (`names.ts`, `Words.tsx`): a name per eye (24 letters, 200 in all), a date (20) and no family name yet (`FAMILY_NAME_LAYOUTS` is empty until an engine draws one). A letter the artwork font cannot draw is refused before checkout: `nameChars.ts` is the font's own cmap as ranges, made by `python scripts/bake_name_chars.py --write` (`--check` runs in the suite `v3picker`, which also holds the page's reading equal to the server's for every code point). Checkout sends the date, the options that applied (swap for two eyes, rotate for three, a Universe look) and `plan8` (the identity of the plan on screen) with the order and knows 409 `plan_changed` and 409 `style_unavailable`.

Run `bash suites/run_main.sh ts v3picker` (the state machine, the rendered components in four languages and the claims scan, the letters). A browser check needs only the dev API: the repository's `scripts/dev_api.py` with the image model stubbed, and a sessionStorage snapshot of eyes sealed by `api/_lib/preview.py protect` from `scripts/styles_tests/synth_iris.py` (a result screen of any eyes opens at once; the way the check of work package WP11 was made).

## Landing page v2 (build, pictures, checks)

The landing page (`src/App.tsx`, everything in `src/landing`) is one honest page: the visitor buys a digital file, every picture of a room, a wall or a hand is an AI visualisation and says so inside the picture, every primary action goes to `/try`, and ordering is announced as "opens soon" until the deployment takes orders (`src/landing/ordering.ts`). All its words are JSON, one file per language (`src/landing/copy/en.json`, `de.json`, `lt.json`, `hu.json`, the same keys; `scripts/check_texts.mjs` checks them), all its prices come through `src/landing/prices.ts` (the visitor's own ladder, price experiments included), all its pictures through the hashed manifest (`src/landing/assets.ts`).

* **Composition** (`src/App.tsx`): the first render is the notice bar, header, hero (the very markup of the prerendered first screen), footer and the sticky phone button. The Reveal, the wall chapter (its size guide and "More ways to see it" are chunks of their own), the styles gallery, how it works, pricing, the close-ups, trust, the FAQ and the closing scene are one chunk each with its own stylesheet (`src/landing/sectionLoaders.ts`): the chunks of all nine are fetched at once right after the first render, but a section is mounted only when its code is here and the main thread has an idle slice, one at a time in page order, or at once when the visitor comes within 1500 px of it (`src/landing/sectionQueue.ts`: left to React.lazy the reveals cluster in tasks of up to 320 ms on a phone); until then an empty slot of the section's height in the page's language (`src/landing/slots.ts`, written by `node scripts/measure_slots.mjs --write`, held by `npm run check:slots`) holds its place and carries its id. ## How a deployment is built on Vercel (what the first Preview of the merged release taught, 2026-10-06)

`vercel.json` says `installCommand: "echo skip"`, `buildCommand: "bash scripts/vercel_build.sh"` and `outputDirectory: "dist"`. That is a rule, not a taste: **no `node_modules` may be in the project folder while Vercel bundles the Python functions.** Vercel makes the bundles of `api/*.py` from the project folder right after the install step, while the build command runs, and a `node_modules` there rides into every bundle (`excludeFiles` did not keep it out). With it the four rendering functions (62 MiB of `api/` plus the Python packages) went over the size limit and every Preview of `v3-release` failed before anything was served; the same tree deployed with the install skipped, with `node_modules` deleted right after the install, and with `api/` cut to 3 MiB. So `scripts/vercel_build.sh` copies the project to a temporary folder, runs `npm ci` and `npm run build` there and copies `dist/` back; the project folder never holds `node_modules`. The build check refuses a `vercel.json` that drops the three lines (`scripts/check_styles.mjs` item 10). Locally nothing changes: `npm run build` is still the build.

What the Previews showed (the commit statuses of `Foxas1995/snapeyes`, `v3-release`): the 11 function entries with their `excludeFiles`, the 3.14 interpreter pin and the pinned `requirements.txt` all deploy; the real page builds on Vercel's Linux with `VERCEL_ENV=preview` (the landing's release gate is empty: every tile names a style the registry can make, so neither a Preview nor a production build stops at it). The sizes of the function bundles are not readable without a login; the estimate stays `node scripts/bundle_report.mjs --pypi` (a rendering function 187.5 MiB, any other 143.7 MiB).

## Environment variables (Vercel)

- `GEMINI_API_KEY` (required)
- `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` (optional, enables the training memory)
- `SNAPEYES_VISION_MODEL` / `SNAPEYES_IMAGE_MODEL` (optional overrides; defaults `gemini-3.8-flash` / `gemini-3.1-flash-image`)
- `STYLE_SLOW_CPU` (optional, 1.0 to 6.0, default 1.6: the slow factor of an instance against a quiet core, from the admin action `cpu_probe`), `STYLE_FUNCTION_MEM_MB` (optional, default 2048: the function memory the style engines budget against) and `STYLE_PLATE_CACHE` / `STYLE_PLATE_CACHE_MB` (optional: the folder and the cap of the 4K plate cache, default `/tmp/snapeyes_plates` and 256)
